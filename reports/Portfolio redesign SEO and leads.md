# Fix the funnel, then add motion

Noman should rebuild the portfolio as a fast, statically rendered sales page for freelance clients, and do it in this order: repair the contact path, move to a custom domain, restructure content into typed MDX collections, and only then add the motion hero. Four findings drive that order. First, the reference hero is not real-time 3D: it is a pre-rendered portrait tilted with pointer-driven transforms in React and Motion, so the look costs almost no runtime and needs no Three.js ([Contra](https://contra.com/p/PvgFOMNO-tested-one-of-motion-sites-premium)). Second, head terms such as "web developer", "AI developer" and even "hire Next.js developer" are not winnable by a new one-person site; marketplaces hold those results and only 1.74% of new pages reach the top 10 within a year ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/)). The realistic search wins are his own name, narrow long-tail phrases and first-hand technical posts. Third, the current site loses every lead it attracts, because the contact form sends nothing and the admin cannot save on Vercel. Fourth, the evidence that design choices lift leads is strong for speed, plain copy and short forms, and weak or absent for 3D, availability badges, pricing and testimonials on freelancer sites. The plan at the end is specific to this repository and marks which steps rest on judgement and not on data.

## The reference hero is a tilted image, not a WebGL scene

Third-party write-ups of the "3D Jack Portfolio" prompt describe a dark full-viewport hero with a gradient headline and a **"magnetic-hover 3D portrait that tracks the cursor in real time"**, followed by a two-row marquee tied to scroll, a character-by-character text reveal, sticky project cards that scale down 3% as the next card slides over, and rounded "peel" transitions between dark and light sections. The build used React and Motion (formerly Framer Motion) inside Lovable ([Contra](https://contra.com/p/PvgFOMNO-tested-one-of-motion-sites-premium)). A public mirror of MotionSites prompts says they are "optimized for React, Tailwind CSS, Framer Motion, and HLS.js", with no mention of Three.js ([GitHub mirror](https://github.com/TheSatyam-Singh/motionsites.ai)). The researchers could not render the page or read the gated prompt, so the exact fonts, colours and copy are unknown, and whether the portrait is a still image, a transparent video or layered images is unconfirmed ([motionsites.ai](https://motionsites.ai/?prompt=3d-jack-portfolio-hero)). Noman should open the page and screenshot it before design work starts.

This matters because it removes the main technical risk. The codebase has no animation or 3D libraries today, and the cheapest faithful route adds only one: Motion, which is MIT-licensed ([motion.dev](https://motion.dev/docs/gsap-vs-motion)). The recipe is a server-rendered H1 with CSS gradient text, a portrait served through `next/image` as the largest paint, a small client component that feeds pointer position into Motion values to rotate the portrait inside a `perspective` wrapper, scroll-linked marquee rows, and project cards using `position: sticky` with a scale transform. None of this needs Tailwind; the existing plain-CSS approach carries over.

Real-time 3D is the wrong default for the hero. One measured Spline case went from **Lighthouse 30 and LCP 5.7 s when loaded eagerly to Lighthouse 90 and LCP 0.6 s after lazy-loading** behind a static thumbnail ([dev.to](https://dev.to/tolumen/optimizing-web-performance-how-lazy-loading-spline-assets-took-our-build-from-30-to-90-in-4ne2)). That is a single self-reported site, and no large study of WebGL heroes exists, but the mechanism is uncontested: when the canvas is the largest element, an empty canvas delays LCP ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgl-three-js-site-seo-rankable-guide)). If Noman wants genuine 3D to prove the skill, it belongs in a case study or a click-to-run demo below the fold, built with React Three Fiber v9 (the React 19 release) ([R3F migration guide](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/tutorials/v9-migration-guide)), loaded with `next/dynamic` after a poster image has painted, and rendered with `frameloop="demand"` to save battery ([R3F docs](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/advanced/scaling-performance)). No source tested R3F, drei or Spline against Next.js 16 with Turbopack, so any real-3D work needs a spike first.

Three constraints shape the motion layer. Cursor tilt has no input on touch devices, so the mobile hero should be static or use a slow CSS float. Reduced motion should swap movement for short fades instead of removing all feedback ([OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/prefers-reduced-motion-accessible-animation/)), and a marquee or looping video still needs a pause control, because the media query alone does not satisfy WCAG 2.2.2 ([A11y Collective](https://www.a11y-collective.com/blog/wcag-animation/)). Page-to-page morphs between the work list and a case study can use React's `ViewTransition`, which the Next.js 16.3.8 docs say works in the App Router with no configuration ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/view-transitions)); the repo is on 16.2.9, whose bundled docs still list an experimental flag ([unpkg next@16.2.7](https://app.unpkg.com/next@16.2.7/files/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/05-config/01-next-config-js/viewTransition.md)), so upgrade Next first or treat this as optional polish. GSAP and Lenis are not needed for this design.

One tension is unresolved by the evidence. The reference uses a stylised 3D portrait, while usability research (seen only through secondary reports) says visitors look closely at photos of real people and skip generic imagery ([CountingWorks citing NN/g](https://www.countingworkspro.com/blog/why-real-photos-not-stock-shots-make-your-firm-instantly-more-trustworthy)). For an offshore freelancer, proof of being a real person carries weight. A reasonable resolution is a stylised portrait of Noman in the hero and a real headshot on the About page and beside the contact form; no test data supports either choice over the other. If the portrait is AI-generated, check the tool's commercial-output terms for the plan used.

## Head terms are out of reach; his name and long tails are not

The results for "hire Next.js developer" style queries are Lemon.io, Turing, Flexiple, Upwork, Arc.dev, Fiverr and Toptal, plus agency guides; no individual portfolio appeared in the researchers' US search ([Lemon.io](https://lemon.io/hire/next-js-developers/), [Turing](https://turing.com/hire/next-js-developers), [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/next.js-freelancers/)). Ahrefs found the average first-ranked page is five years old and 72.9% of top-10 pages are over three years old ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/)). Google's John Mueller told a new site owner in January 2026 that "being visible in popular search results is not the first step" and advised direct promotion first ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-mueller-free-subdomain-hosting-makes-seo-harder/565249/)). A redesign will not put this site on page one for "web developer", "AI developer" or "3D web developer", and the report should not promise it.

What is winnable falls into three tiers, and this tiering is the researchers' judgement from SERP composition, not measured data. No keyword volumes or difficulty scores could be obtained, and Australian results were not inspected. Branded queries ("Noman E Jawad", "Noman Jawad developer") are winnable within weeks and matter most, because prospects from LinkedIn, Upwork and referrals search the name. Narrow three-part phrases that combine stack, problem and audience are winnable in 3 to 12 months with good pages: examples to validate in Google Keyword Planner include "headless WooCommerce Next.js developer", "migrate WordPress to Next.js developer", "freelance React Three Fiber developer" and "WooCommerce developer for Australian businesses". Specific technical posts on fresh problems are winnable opportunistically. Each of these phrases likely draws tens of searches a month; the value is intent, not volume.

### A custom domain is the first SEO action

No Google statement says `vercel.app` is penalised, and no controlled study compares it with a custom domain. The case rests on Mueller's general remark that free subdomain hosting "attracts a lot of spam & low-effort content" ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-mueller-free-subdomain-hosting-makes-seo-harder/565249/)), on platform risk (one user's old `vercel.app` name was reassigned to someone else after a project was deleted, and Google kept treating it as canonical ([Vercel Community](https://community.vercel.com/t/vercel-subdomain-reassignment-causing-google-search-console-canonical-url-conflict/36418))), and on the fact that authority resets on any later move. With seven pages and little ranking equity, moving now costs almost nothing. Google's site-move guidance calls for permanent redirects kept at least a year, updated canonicals, a new sitemap and the Change of Address tool ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes)). Keywords in the domain have minimal effect ([Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)), so a name-based domain on a mainstream TLD is the right pick.

### Technical SEO is a one-off job that buys eligibility, not rankings

Next.js 16 covers the baseline natively: `metadataBase` in the root layout, `alternates.canonical` per page, `sitemap.ts`, `robots.ts`, and `opengraph-image.tsx` generated at build time ([Next.js metadata](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/generate-metadata), [Next.js OG image](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/file-conventions/metadata/opengraph-image)). Three current habits in the repo deliver nothing. The meta keywords list is ignored: "Google Search doesn't use the keywords meta tag" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)). Sitemap `priority` and `changefreq` are ignored, and `lastmod` counts only when accurate ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/06/sitemaps-lastmod-ping)). FAQ rich results stopped appearing for all sites from 7 May 2026 ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage)). The existing Person and WebSite JSON-LD should gain a `ProfilePage` wrapper on the About page, which Google documents for "about me" pages ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page)), and `BlogPosting` on posts. No evidence quantifies a ranking gain from any of this schema for an individual.

The larger structural gain is more URLs. Today one home page competes for every phrase. Four to six hand-built service pages (Next.js builds, WooCommerce and headless commerce, AI integration, 3D web experiences, Laravel), a real `/work` index, `/about` and `/contact` give Google distinct entry points, with each case study linking to the service it proves. The limit is firm: Google lists "pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)), so one Australia-focused page with distinct content is defensible and a set of city pages is not. Google also warns that generating many pages with AI "without adding value" breaches its scaled-content policy ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)); AI as editor over first-hand material is within policy.

### AI search rewards mentions elsewhere, not files on the site

Google states there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode" and no special schema or files ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). An Ahrefs study of 75,000 brands found AI visibility correlates most with YouTube mentions (about 0.74) and branded web mentions (about 0.66 to 0.71), and barely with page count (about 0.19) ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/)); the sample was established brands, so it applies to a solo developer only indirectly. Reports that Google ignores `llms.txt` were seen only in secondary summaries, so treat the file as harmless and low priority. The practical work is server-rendering all text, leaving AI crawlers unblocked in `robots.ts`, keeping name and description identical across the site, GitHub, LinkedIn, npm and Upwork, and earning real mentions: client-site credit links, one or two small open-source projects, and listings on the marketplaces and directories that already rank for hire-intent terms. A Google Business Profile does not fit a remote-only freelancer, since the guidelines require in-person customer contact ([Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)).

A realistic 12-month outlook, which is a judgement and not a forecast: ownership of the branded results, tens to low hundreds of non-branded clicks a month mostly to technical posts, a few long-tail hire-intent rankings and occasional inbound enquiries. Search is unlikely to be the main lead source in year one. Because 99.2% of queries that trigger AI Overviews are informational ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks/)), the blog earns its keep through credibility, links and material to send prospects, more than through traffic.

## Typed MDX in git replaces the admin that cannot save

The current admin writes `content/content.json` with `fs.writeFile`, which does not persist on Vercel. The durable pattern on Vercel is to make each edit a Git commit, so the deployment itself is the publish step. The recommended stack is MDX and YAML files in the repo, validated by Zod schemas through Content Collections, with Keystatic as an optional browser admin over the same files. Compatibility is declared, not tested: `@content-collections/next` lists Next 12 to 16 as peers ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@content-collections/next)), and `@keystatic/next` lists Next 14+ and React 18.2 or 19 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@keystatic/next)). Keystatic's GitHub mode commits edits through a GitHub App and restricts login to users with write access to the repo ([Keystatic](https://keystatic.com/docs/github-mode)); its cloud tier is free for up to three users ([Keystatic Cloud](https://keystatic.com/docs/cloud)). Nobody installed this stack against the repo on Next 16.2.9 with Turbopack, and Keystatic is still a 0.x release, so the first task is a half-day spike.

The exact React pin narrows the alternatives. `react` is fixed at 19.0.0, while Sanity's Next toolkit needs React 19.2.3 or later ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/next-sanity)), Fumadocs MDX needs 19.2.0 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/fumadocs-mdx)), and Payload's Next adapter requires Next 16.3.3 or later ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@payloadcms/next)). Contentlayer is unmaintained ([GitHub](https://github.com/contentlayerdev/contentlayer)). Sanity's free plan is generous but moves content out of the repo and has no scheduled publishing ([Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/pricing)); Supabase's free tier pauses projects after a week of inactivity ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing)), a real hazard for a low-traffic static site. If Noman does not want a browser admin at all, writing MDX in the editor with a `pnpm new:post` scaffold script is equally valid and removes a dependency. One more caveat: with `@next/mdx` under Turbopack, plugins must have serialisable options ([Next.js MDX guide](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/mdx)), which is why compiling MDX in the content layer is the safer path for code highlighting with `rehype-pretty-code`.

The single JSON file should split into collections, following the researchers' proposed model (a design recommendation, with no external standard behind it). Posts live at `content/blog/<slug>/index.mdx`, case studies at `content/work/<slug>/index.mdx`, services at `content/services/<slug>.mdx`, testimonials as one YAML file each, and the hero, about, contact and navigation copy as singletons under `content/site/`. Images sit beside the entry that uses them. The slug comes from the folder name, the six existing `/work/[slug]` URLs stay identical, and relations (post to project, project to service) are stored on one side and validated at build so a typo fails the build. Required fields for description and cover alt text are what stop SEO regressions when content is added often. All queries go through `src/lib/content/` so pages never touch storage directly.

Assets need only light discipline at this scale. Vercel's Hobby plan includes 5,000 image transformations a month, after which new images return HTTP 402 ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/image-optimization/limits-and-pricing)); tens of statically imported images sit well inside that. In Next.js 16 the `priority` prop on `next/image` is deprecated in favour of `preload` or `fetchPriority="high"` ([Next.js Image](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image)), which applies to the hero portrait. Long case-study videos belong on a video host, not in `/public`. One plan-level question sits outside the code: Vercel's Hobby plan excludes commercial use, defined to include "advertising the sale of a product or service" ([Vercel fair use](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)). A portfolio that sells services and captures leads plausibly qualifies. Only Vercel can rule on it, and Noman should read the definition and decide before launch.

## Speed, plain words and a working form have data; 3D does not

The strongest conversion evidence is general web research, not portfolio research; no 2025 to 2026 primary study of freelance developer portfolios was found. Within that limit, three levers are well supported. Visitors decide in about 10 seconds whether to stay, based on Microsoft Research data covering more than two billion dwell times ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). Landing pages written at a 5th to 7th grade reading level convert at 11.1%, about double professional-level copy at 5.3%, across 41,000 pages ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/)). On lead-generation sites, a 0.1 s mobile speed gain produced 21.6% more form submissions in a 2019 Google and Deloitte study of large brands ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions)). By contrast, only vendors claim that 3D heroes raise conversion, and none published controlled data ([Mindactive](https://mindactive.com/?p=14347)). A survey of about 60 hiring managers found 93% would look at a portfolio but 51% said its absence would not hurt a candidate ([Scrimba](https://scrimba.com/articles/how-to-build-a-web-developer-portfolio-that-gets-you-hired/)). The site therefore matters most as a sales page for freelance clients and as a tie-breaker for employers, and the motion hero should be judged as brand polish that must not cost speed.

The design consequences follow directly. The hero needs one short sentence saying what he builds and for whom, one proof point, one primary action ("Start a project") and one secondary ("See work"), in words a non-technical business owner reads at a glance. The current IDE-style shell, with its sidebar, topbar and command palette, speaks to developers and hides the pitch from clients; replacing it with a conventional top navigation of about five items and a call-to-action button is a judgement call, and the command palette can survive as an extra. Light and dark themes should both ship, since lab studies show light mode reads better for most people with normal vision ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)); the reference is dark-first, so the dark and light "peel" sections give a natural way to keep body text on light backgrounds. A bento grid for proof (stack, years, outcomes, availability) is cheap, server-rendered CSS, though the trend sources behind it are low-authority blogs.

The contact path deserves more care than any visual. The supported form shape is name, email and one message box, because extra text areas and dropdowns are linked to clearly lower completion ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/6746/which-types-of-form-fields-lower-landing-page-conversions.aspx)). A plain email address and a booking link should sit beside it. Reply speed is one of the best-documented levers in lead conversion: firms that responded within an hour were about seven times as likely to qualify a lead in a study of 2.24 million B2B leads ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)); that research covers sales teams, so read it for direction only. Testimonials have only single-site tests behind them, such as a 34% lift when one site moved three testimonials higher ([Wishpond](https://wishpond.com/blog/10-easy-ab-tests-to-help-increase-conversions)). The hidden Testimonials section should go live only with real, named, attributable quotes; if the current entries are placeholders, publishing them would damage trust.

Case studies are the strongest asset and need content work more than design work. Each should open with a summary strip (client and country, his role, stack, timeline, one to three result numbers, live link), tell problem, approach and result in the client's terms, and close with a call to action. Where client metrics are unavailable, Lighthouse and Core Web Vitals before and after are measurable alone. This structure is practitioner consensus without test data. The same holds for "from" pricing and specialist positioning, which rest on weak surveys and one consultant's account of fewer but better enquiries ([pravinkumar.co](https://www.pravinkumar.co/blog/why-i-publish-my-pricing-solo-consultant-2026)). For the offshore trust gap no data exists at all; the reasoned approach is to state Dhaka and the working-hours overlap with Australian and US clients openly, name the process (weekly demo, staging link, milestone payments), and let the written quality of the case studies stand as the communication sample.

Expectations should be sized honestly. Service sites typically convert a low single-digit share of visitors into enquiries ([Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/form-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026/)), so at portfolio traffic levels A/B testing is impractical. Track every contact action as an event and compare periods of four to eight weeks instead.

## A six-phase plan for this repository

The phases are ordered by expected return per hour. Durations are estimates for one developer working part-time and are not sourced.

| Phase | Goal | Estimated effort |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Stop losing leads and remove stale or exposed files | 1 to 2 days |
| 1 | Custom domain and SEO baseline | 2 to 3 days |
| 2 | Content architecture and blog | 1 to 2 weeks |
| 3 | Redesign with motion hero | 2 to 3 weeks |
| 4 | Service pages and deeper case studies | 1 to 2 weeks, mostly writing |
| 5 | Publishing, off-page work and measurement | Ongoing |

**Phase 0: stop the leaks.** Replace the fake submit in `src/components/client/ContactForm.tsx` with a server action that validates input with Zod, checks a honeypot and a Cloudflare Turnstile token, and sends through Resend: one notification to Noman with reply-to set to the visitor, and one acknowledgement to the visitor. Turnstile's free plan has unlimited challenges ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/plans/)); Resend's free allowance was reported by third-party trackers only, so confirm it on Resend's own pricing page. Resend needs a verified sending domain, so if the domain is not yet bought, point the form at Web3Forms (250 free submissions a month ([Web3Forms](https://web3forms.com/pricing))) as a same-day stopgap. Show the plain email address as a fallback and keep the typed message on error. In the same pass, delete `public/legacy/` so the old `admin.html` is no longer publicly reachable; update "Available for freelance · April 2026" and "Last updated · Apr 2026" in `content/content.json`, and make the "last updated" value derive from build time so it cannot go stale again; remove the meta keywords list from `src/app/layout.tsx`; and add Vercel Analytics custom events for CTA clicks, form start, form success, form error, email clicks and CV downloads, using the `@vercel/analytics` package already installed. Render the Testimonials section and QuoteCarousel on the home page only after confirming the quotes are real and attributable.

**Phase 1: domain and SEO baseline.** Register a name-based domain, add it to the existing Vercel project as primary, and set the `vercel.app` production host to redirect permanently to it. Do not delete or rename the Vercel project, because the subdomain can be reassigned. Create one `SITE_URL` constant (for example in `src/lib/site.ts`, read from an environment variable) and replace every hard-coded `https://nomanjawad.vercel.app` in `layout.tsx`, `robots.ts`, `sitemap.ts` and the JSON-LD. Set `metadataBase` and a title template in the root layout, and set `alternates.canonical` on each page individually, including `work/[slug]`; a canonical set only in the root layout would be inherited and point every page at the home page. Add a root `opengraph-image.tsx` and a per-case-study version. Give `sitemap.ts` real `lastModified` dates and drop `priority` and `changeFrequency`. Verify the new domain and the old host in Google Search Console, submit the sitemap, run Change of Address, and import the site into Bing Webmaster Tools. Update the site link on GitHub, LinkedIn, npm and Upwork. Decide the Hobby-versus-Pro question here.

**Phase 2: content architecture.** Start with a spike that installs Content Collections and Keystatic on a branch and confirms both build under Next 16.2.9 with Turbopack and React 19.0.0. If Keystatic misbehaves, proceed with files and a scaffold script only. Then write a one-off script that splits `content/content.json` into `content/site/*.yaml` singletons, `content/work/<slug>/index.mdx` entries with identical slugs, and testimonial files; replace `src/lib/types.ts` and `src/lib/content.ts` with Zod schemas and typed queries under `src/lib/content/`. Add `src/app/(site)/blog/page.tsx` and `blog/[slug]/page.tsx` with `generateStaticParams` and `dynamicParams = false`, matching the existing work route, plus an RSS route handler and per-post OG images, and extend `sitemap.ts` to read from the same queries. Once Keystatic works in GitHub mode, delete `src/app/admin/` and `src/app/api/content/route.ts`. The current Basic Auth in `src/proxy.ts` does protect those routes today, so this is a persistence fix and not an emergency; after removal, either retire the proxy matcher or re-scope it, testing that it does not interfere with Keystatic's GitHub OAuth callback. Mark `/keystatic` as noindex and disallow it in `robots.ts`.

**Phase 3: redesign.** Screenshot the reference first. Add `motion` as the only new UI dependency. Break `src/app/globals.css` into design tokens (colour, type scale, spacing, radii for both themes) plus per-component CSS Modules, so the 409-line file stops growing; this is a maintainability suggestion, not a requirement. Replace the IDE shell with a top navigation and a persistent call-to-action button, with a sticky bottom action bar on mobile. Build the hero as a Server Component containing the H1, sub-line, both CTAs and the portrait through `next/image` with `preload`, and wrap only the tilt behaviour in a small client component gated on `(pointer: fine)` and `prefers-reduced-motion`. Drive the tilt with Motion values, not React state, to avoid re-renders. Add the scroll-tied marquee with a pause control, a word-level text reveal that keeps the full sentence available to screen readers, sticky stacking project cards and a bento proof grid. Reassess whether Instrument Serif and JetBrains Mono suit a client-facing page; monospace body text reinforces the developer-tool feel the redesign is moving away from. Hold a budget of LCP under 2.5 s, INP under 200 ms and CLS under 0.1 ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals)), tested on a throttled mid-range phone, and check in Search Console's URL Inspection that the rendered HTML contains the H1, copy and links.

**Phase 4: pages that sell.** Add `/work` as an index, `/about` with a real photo and ProfilePage JSON-LD, `/contact`, and four to six `/services/[slug]` pages, each with scope, process, two or three linked case studies and a call to action. Rewrite the six case studies from 140-character strings into full MDX with the summary strip, measured outcomes and a closing CTA; mark three as featured and order them by relevance to the target buyer. Add a Cal.com booking link as a button or popup, not an inline embed, since inline embeds can auto-scroll the page on load ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/mohymenul/troubleshooting-automatic-scrolling-to-calcom-inline-embed-on-page-load-28g3)). State location, overlap hours and a reply-time promise he will keep. Add "from" pricing only if he is comfortable with fewer, better-qualified enquiries; the evidence is one consultant's account.

**Phase 5: sustain.** Publish at a pace he can hold for a year; the researchers suggest about two posts a month, weighted toward real client problems and client-decision topics such as WordPress versus Next.js, but no data sets a required cadence. Cross-post to dev.to and Hashnode after the original is indexed, accepting that Google does not treat canonical tags on syndicated copies as reliable ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting)). Ask clients for footer credit links and LinkedIn recommendations, publish one small open-source project, and list on the marketplaces and directories that rank for hire-intent terms. Review Search Console monthly for queries sitting at positions 8 to 20, send a real test submission through the form each month, and add a "how did you find me?" field, since referrals from AI assistants and private messages rarely show in analytics.

## Conclusion

The request framed the project as a design and SEO problem, and the research reframes it as a funnel problem with a design layer on top. The motion hero Noman wants turns out to be the cheapest part: one image, one small library and careful loading. The expensive parts are the ones with no visual payoff, which are a working contact path, a domain he owns, content he can add without touching code, and case studies that state outcomes. Those are also the parts with the strongest evidence behind them.

The honest ceiling is that search will not carry this site in its first year, and no redesign changes that. The site's commercial job is to convert visitors who arrive from LinkedIn, Upwork, referrals and outreach, so outreach links should land on the most relevant case study or service page. Two things remain unknown and are worth resolving early: the real appearance of the reference page, which nobody on the research side could render, and whether Vercel regards the site as commercial. Both can be settled in an afternoon, before any code is written.
