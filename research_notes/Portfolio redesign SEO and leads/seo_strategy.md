# SEO strategy for a solo freelance developer portfolio (Noman E Jawad), as of October 2026

Research date: 2026-10-05. Source-quality note: Google Search Central and Next.js docs were fetched directly and are primary. Several third-party statistics (marked "snippet only") were seen only in search-result summaries and could not be opened; treat those as less certain. No keyword-tool data (Ahrefs/Semrush volume or KD) could be obtained; see Gaps under question 1.

## 1. How realistic are head terms, what do SERPs contain, and which keywords are winnable?

### Takeaway
Head terms ("web developer", "full stack developer", "AI developer") and even generic commercial terms ("hire Next.js developer") are not realistically winnable by a new one-person site: the results are held by marketplaces, talent platforms and agencies, and Ahrefs data shows very few new pages reach the top 10 within a year. The winnable ground is branded queries (his name), narrow stack-plus-problem-plus-audience long tails, and informational/technical posts where he has first-hand experience.

### Cited Findings
- Only 1.74% of newly published pages rank in the top 10 within a year (down from 5.7% in 2017); 6.11% when restricted to non-empty English content. The average #1 page is 5 years old and 72.9% of top-10 pages are more than 3 years old. Study updated 15 May 2025, 1M random URLs. — [Ahrefs: How long does it take to rank](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/)
- 96.55% of pages in Ahrefs' index (~14 billion pages) get zero Google traffic; pages that get traffic without backlinks are almost all on low-competition topics. (Study dated 2023.) — [Ahrefs search traffic study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-traffic-study/)
- Observed result set for "hire next.js developer" style queries (via a US web search tool, not a logged Google SERP): Lemon.io, Flexiple, Turing, Upwork, Arc.dev, Fiverr, Toptal, plus agency "how to hire" guides (SolGuruz, Adaface, Space-O). No individual freelancer portfolio appeared. — [Lemon.io](https://lemon.io/hire/next-js-developers/), [Turing](https://turing.com/hire/next-js-developers), [Flexiple](https://flexiple.com/nextjs), [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/next.js-freelancers/), [Arc.dev](https://arc.dev/hire-developers/nextjs), [Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/hire/nextjs), [Toptal React](https://www.toptal.com/developers/react)
- John Mueller (Jan 2026) to a new site owner: "You're publishing content on a topic that's already been extremely well covered" and "Being visible in popular search results is not the first step to becoming a useful & popular web presence"; he advised community-building and direct promotion first. — [Search Engine Journal, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-mueller-free-subdomain-hosting-makes-seo-harder/565249/)
- Google's doorway-abuse policy explicitly lists "Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as spam. This constrains location-keyword pages. — [Google spam policies, updated 28 Aug 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- A freelancer-portfolio writeup claims first-page rankings came from an exact-keyword H1, FAQ section, live project demos and separate blog pages for long-tail terms, and says getting a custom domain earlier and adding location-keyword posts would have helped. This is a single self-reported account, low evidential weight (snippet only). — [pavankiran.is-a.dev](https://pavankiran.is-a.dev/blog/portfolio-website-that-gets-hired)
- 99.2% of keywords that trigger AI Overviews are informational (Ahrefs, Apr 2025), meaning informational blog traffic is the most exposed to click loss, while commercial/hire-intent queries are less affected. — [Ahrefs: AI Overviews reduce clicks](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks/)

### Inferences
- Realistic tiering (inference from the SERP composition and Ahrefs data above, not measured):
  - Not winnable: "web developer", "full stack developer", "AI developer", "Next.js developer", "hire Next.js developer", "freelance web developer". Dominated by marketplaces, job boards, Wikipedia-type definitional pages and agencies with thousands of referring domains.
  - Winnable within weeks: branded queries. "Noman E Jawad", "Noman Jawad developer", "nomanjawad". This matters because referrals, Upwork/LinkedIn prospects and AI assistants look up the name; owning that SERP (site, LinkedIn, GitHub, dev.to) is the highest-certainty SEO win.
  - Winnable in 3-12 months with good pages: narrow three-part long tails (stack + problem/vertical + audience/geo).
  - Winnable opportunistically: specific technical how-to posts on fresh or niche problems (new Next.js 16 features, R3F performance, WooCommerce headless edge cases) where few authoritative pages exist yet.
- Example keyword clusters to validate in a keyword tool (volumes unknown, likely tens of searches per month each; the value is intent, not volume):
  - Next.js hire-intent: "freelance Next.js developer for startups", "Next.js developer for hire remote", "Next.js App Router migration consultant", "migrate WordPress to Next.js developer", "Next.js performance optimisation freelancer".
  - 3D web: "freelance React Three Fiber developer", "Three.js developer for product configurator", "3D website developer for hire", "WebGL landing page developer".
  - AI integration: "AI integration developer for small business", "add AI chatbot to website developer", "RAG chatbot developer freelance", "OpenAI/Claude API integration Next.js developer".
  - WooCommerce/WordPress + geo: "WooCommerce developer for Australian businesses", "headless WooCommerce Next.js developer", "WooCommerce speed optimisation freelancer", "Laravel developer remote Australia timezone".
  - Offshore/value framing: "hire full stack developer Bangladesh", "remote full stack developer Dhaka", "affordable Next.js developer overlap with AEST".
  - Comparison/decision content (cited more often by AI engines; see question 6): "Next.js vs WordPress for small business site", "headless WooCommerce vs Shopify cost", "freelancer vs agency for Next.js build".
- Geo pages are only safe when each has genuinely distinct content (e.g. one Australia page with AUD pricing, timezone overlap, Australian client case studies, GST/payment-gateway specifics). A set of near-identical "/developer-in-sydney", "/developer-in-melbourne" pages matches Google's doorway example.
- Because he is in Dhaka targeting Australia/US, local-pack SEO is not available (see question 7 on Google Business Profile); geo relevance must come from content and client proof, not location signals.

### Gaps
- No search volume or keyword difficulty figures were obtainable for any of the target terms. Public articles do not publish them and the tools are paywalled. All clusters above need validating in Google Keyword Planner (free), Ahrefs/Semrush free checkers, and later in Search Console query data.
- No reliable published case study with data of a freelance developer portfolio ranking for hire-intent terms was found; available accounts are anecdotal and self-reported.
- Actual Google SERPs for AU (google.com.au) were not inspected; the search tool used is US-only.

## 2. Does a vercel.app subdomain hurt SEO versus a custom domain? How to migrate?

### Takeaway
There is no Google statement that vercel.app specifically is penalised, but Google's John Mueller said in January 2026 that free subdomain hosting makes SEO harder because of spammy "flatmates", and the practical risks (no ownership of the hostname, authority reset on any move, weaker client trust) make a custom domain the first action item. Migrate now, while the site has almost nothing to lose, using permanent redirects and the Search Console Change of Address tool.

### Cited Findings
- Mueller: "A free subdomain hosting service attracts a lot of spam & low-effort content"; "you're basically opening up shop on a site that's filled with – potentially – problematic 'flatmates'". He did not say ranking is impossible. Context was a different free-subdomain provider (Digitalplat) on the Public Suffix List, not Vercel. The article also notes Gary Illyes' earlier warning about very cheap TLDs. — [Search Engine Journal, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-mueller-free-subdomain-hosting-makes-seo-harder/565249/)
- Keywords in a domain name have minimal ranking effect; TLD matters only for country targeting. — [Google SEO Starter Guide, updated 10 Dec 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
- If a production site is reachable at both project.vercel.app and a custom domain, search engines can treat them as separate pages. Vercel recommends canonical tags pointing at the custom host, an `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on the vercel.app host, and updating environment variables so sitemaps, metadata, JSON-LD and Open Graph do not emit vercel.app URLs (snippet only). — [Vercel KB: avoiding duplicate content with vercel.app URLs](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/avoiding-duplicate-content-with-vercel-app-urls)
- A developer who went the other way (custom domain expired, moved to name.vercel.app) reported Google treated the vercel.app host as a brand-new site with authority reset; re-indexing took 1-2 weeks and rankings began returning in weeks 3-4 after Search Console verification, sitemap submission and manual indexing requests. Anecdotal. — [dev.to: From custom domain to Vercel subdomain](https://dev.to/imranparthib/from-custom-domain-to-vercel-subdomain-how-i-recovered-my-seo-rankings-56i7)
- A Vercel community report (Mar 2026): after a user moved to a custom domain and deleted the original project, Vercel reassigned the old vercel.app subdomain to another user and Google kept treating the old subdomain as canonical; Vercel staff could not help. — [Vercel Community thread](https://community.vercel.com/t/vercel-subdomain-reassignment-causing-google-search-console-canonical-url-conflict/36418)
- Google's site-move guidance: use server-side permanent redirects (301/308); update rel=canonical to the new URLs; submit the new sitemap; use the Change of Address tool when "moving from one domain or subdomain to another"; "Keep the redirects for as long as possible, generally at least 1 year"; "Expect temporary fluctuation in site ranking during the move"; a medium-sized site takes "a few weeks or more". — [Google: Site move with URL changes](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes)
- Google site names are supported at domain and subdomain level but not subdirectories. — [Google Search Central blog: update on site names](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/07/sitenames)

### Inferences
- Recommended migration sequence: (1) register a name-based domain on a mainstream TLD (e.g. nomanjawad.com or .dev; avoid ultra-cheap TLDs per the Illyes/Mueller comments); (2) add it to the same Vercel project and make it the primary domain; (3) set the vercel.app production domain to permanently redirect (308) to the custom domain rather than serving noindex, since Google's site-move doc prefers redirects and a redirect passes signals while noindex does not; (4) set `metadataBase`, canonical, sitemap, robots, JSON-LD and OG URLs from one `SITE_URL` constant; (5) verify a Domain property for the new domain and a URL-prefix property for the vercel.app host in Search Console, then run Change of Address; (6) update every external profile link (GitHub, LinkedIn, npm, dev.to, Upwork); (7) never delete or rename the Vercel project, because the subdomain can be reassigned and the redirect lost.
- With seven pages and little or no existing ranking equity, the migration risk is negligible and the cost is roughly USD 10-15 per year, so this is the highest-priority action. Every month of content and links built on the vercel.app host increases the later migration cost.
- A custom domain also enables a branded email address and a Search Console Domain property, and is a trust signal for clients paying invoices; these are business rather than ranking benefits.
- Preview deployments (`*-git-*.vercel.app`) should stay non-indexable; Vercel is understood to add noindex to preview URLs by default, but this was not verified in this research.

### Gaps
- No controlled study comparing rankings of vercel.app subdomains with custom domains was found. The case against the subdomain rests on Mueller's general statement, platform-risk anecdotes and reasoning, not on measured ranking differences.
- Whether vercel.app is on the Public Suffix List (which would mean Google treats each subdomain as a separate site) was not verified here; it is believed to be listed.

## 3. Next.js 16 App Router technical SEO

### Takeaway
Next.js 16 (docs at v16.3.8) covers nearly all technical SEO natively: the Metadata API with `metadataBase` and `alternates.canonical`, file conventions for `sitemap.ts`, `robots.ts` and `opengraph-image.tsx`, and JSON-LD rendered as a plain script tag in server components. For a small static portfolio the technical work is a one-off of a day or two; it is necessary for eligibility but will not by itself produce rankings.

### Cited Findings
- Metadata: export a static `metadata` object or an async `generateMetadata` from `layout` or `page`; both are "only supported in Server Components". In v16, `params` and `searchParams` are Promises and must be awaited. `fetch` calls inside `generateMetadata` are memoised with the page's own fetches. Docs version 16.3.8, updated 25 Aug 2026. — [Next.js generateMetadata reference](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/generate-metadata)
- `metadataBase` is "typically set in root app/layout.js"; relative URL fields are composed against it, and "Using a relative path in a URL-based metadata field without configuring a metadataBase will cause a build error". `alternates: { canonical: '/' }` emits `<link rel="canonical">`. — [Next.js generateMetadata reference](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/generate-metadata)
- Streaming metadata (since v15.2): metadata from `generateMetadata` can be streamed after the initial UI for dynamic pages, but Next.js detects "HTML-limited bots" by user agent and serves them metadata in the head; `htmlLimitedBots` in next.config overrides the list. If the page is prerenderable and `generateMetadata` adds no dynamic behaviour, metadata is in the initial HTML. — [Next.js generateMetadata reference](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/generate-metadata)
- `themeColor`, `colorScheme` and `viewport` inside `metadata` are deprecated since v14 in favour of the `viewport` export. The API still supports a `keywords` field that emits `<meta name="keywords">`. — [Next.js generateMetadata reference](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/generate-metadata)
- "Google Search doesn't use the keywords meta tag." — [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
- `app/sitemap.ts` default-exports a function returning `MetadataRoute.Sitemap` (fields: `url`, `lastModified`, `changeFrequency`, `priority`, `alternates.languages`, `images`, `videos`); it is "a special Route Handler that is cached by default". v16 change: the `id` passed with `generateSitemaps` is now a Promise. — [Next.js sitemap.xml reference](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/file-conventions/metadata/sitemap)
- Google does not use `changefreq` or `priority`; it uses `lastmod` only if it is consistently accurate. — [Google Search Central blog, June 2023](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/06/sitemaps-lastmod-ping)
- `opengraph-image` / `twitter-image`: either a static file (.jpg/.png/.gif, with optional `opengraph-image.alt.txt`; OG max 8MB, Twitter max 5MB or the build fails) or code using `ImageResponse` from `next/og` with `alt`, `size` (1200x630 in the docs example) and `contentType` exports. Generated images are "statically optimized (generated at build time and cached)" by default. v16 change: `params` is a Promise. — [Next.js opengraph-image reference](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/file-conventions/metadata/opengraph-image)
- JSON-LD: "Our current recommendation for JSON-LD is to render structured data as a `<script>` tag in your layout.js or page.js components", using `JSON.stringify(jsonLd).replace(/</g, '\\u003c')` to prevent XSS; use a native `<script>`, not `next/script`; type with `schema-dts`; validate with the Rich Results Test and Schema Markup Validator. — [Next.js JSON-LD guide, v16.3.8](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/json-ld)
- ProfilePage structured data is valid for "An 'About Me' page on a blog site"; required: `mainEntity` (Person or Organization) and `name`; recommended: `alternateName`, `description`, `image`, `sameAs`, `dateCreated`, `dateModified`. The associated feature is Discussions and Forums / creator understanding, not a guaranteed rich result. — [Google: ProfilePage structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page)
- FAQ rich results are gone for all sites: Google's FAQ documentation states the feature stopped appearing from 7 May 2026 and the documentation was removed in June 2026. Secondary reports say Google still reads FAQPage markup to understand pages and it need not be removed (not verified against a Google primary source). — [Google FAQPage docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage); [TechWyse report](https://www.techwyse.com/news/ai-search/google-faq-rich-results-deprecated-2026)
- Service schema is not among Google's supported rich-result types and yields no rich result (forum source, low authority, but consistent with Google's search gallery). — [Local Search Forum](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/schema-questions-for-site-replatform-redesign.61922/)
- "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features. There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add." (Google on AI Overviews / AI Mode.) — [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- Core Web Vitals targets: LCP within 2.5s, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1. Google says they align with what its "core ranking systems seek to reward" but gives no guarantee, and warns owners "should not focus on only one or two aspects of page experience". — [Google: Core Web Vitals, updated 10 Dec 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals); [Google: Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

### Inferences
- Minimum technical checklist for this site (inference from the docs above):
  - Root layout: `metadataBase: new URL(SITE_URL)`, `title: { default, template: '%s | Noman E Jawad' }`, description, `alternates.canonical`, `openGraph`, `twitter`, `robots`. Each case study and post sets its own `alternates.canonical` (a canonical set only in the root layout would be inherited by every page and point them all at the home page).
  - `app/sitemap.ts` generated from the same data source as case studies and posts, with real `lastModified` dates; omit `priority` and `changeFrequency` since Google ignores them.
  - `app/robots.ts` allowing all, pointing to the sitemap on the custom domain; do not block AI crawlers if AI visibility is a goal.
  - `app/opengraph-image.tsx` plus per-route images for case studies and posts (title, stack, result metric). OG images affect share click-through on LinkedIn/X/Slack, not rankings.
  - JSON-LD: home/about = `WebSite` (with `name` for site name) + `ProfilePage` whose `mainEntity` is a `Person` (`name`, `jobTitle`, `image`, `knowsAbout`, `address` country, `sameAs` to GitHub/LinkedIn/npm/dev.to/Upwork); blog posts = `BlogPosting` with `author` referencing the same Person `@id`, `datePublished`, `dateModified`; case studies = `Article` or `CreativeWork`; `BreadcrumbList` on nested pages. `Service` and `FAQPage` are optional, harmless and produce no Google rich result in 2026; do not spend time expecting visual SERP gains from them.
  - Rendering: keep all content pages statically prerendered (use `generateStaticParams` for `[slug]` routes); ISR is unnecessary for a site redeployed on each content change. Ensure 3D/WebGL sections are client components loaded lazily with real HTML text content around them, because canvas content is not indexable text and heavy Three.js bundles are the main threat to LCP and INP on this type of site.
  - Use `next/image` with explicit dimensions and meaningful `alt`; mark only the hero image as priority/preload.
  - RSS: no built-in convention; implement as a route handler (`app/feed.xml/route.ts`) and advertise via `alternates.types['application/rss+xml']`. Useful for dev.to/Hashnode import and feed readers, not a ranking factor.
- With the `cacheComponents` model in Next.js 16, check that `generateMetadata` on dynamic routes does not unintentionally become request-time; the docs note an error is raised when metadata is the only dynamic part of an otherwise prerenderable page.
- Core Web Vitals function as a tie-breaker; for a portfolio they matter more for client impression (a slow developer portfolio undermines the sales pitch) than for ranking.

### Gaps
- The `next/image`, `robots.ts`, `generateStaticParams` and Cache Components docs pages were not fetched individually; the related checklist items rest on general knowledge of the framework rather than quoted v16 docs.
- No Google primary source was fetched confirming that FAQPage markup is still used for understanding after the rich-result removal.
- No evidence was found quantifying ranking gains from Person/ProfilePage schema for individuals.

## 4. On-page SEO and E-E-A-T for an individual

### Takeaway
Google's own guidance reduces on-page work to accurate, unique titles, descriptive URLs, anchor text and alt text, and people-first content; heading order, word count and meta keywords do not affect ranking. For an individual, the differentiator is demonstrable first-hand experience and trust: a real name, photo, verifiable client work, and consistent identity across profiles.

### Cited Findings
- Titles should be unique, clear and concise and can include the business name and location. Heading order does not affect rankings (it helps accessibility). "There's no magical word count target, minimum or maximum." Duplicate URLs are inefficient but not penalised. Descriptive words in URLs are recommended. Alt text should be short and descriptive; anchor text should describe the target. — [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
- "Keyword stuffing is against Google's spam policies." — [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
- Is E-E-A-T a ranking factor? "No, it's not" (Starter Guide); "While E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor, using a mix of factors that can identify content with good E-E-A-T is useful"; "Of these aspects, trust is most important." — [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide); [Google: Creating helpful content, updated 1 Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- "We strongly encourage adding accurate authorship information, such as bylines to content where readers might expect it." Google frames quality around Who created it, How, and Why. — [Google: Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Changes can take from hours to several months to show; generally wait a few weeks to assess. — [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
- Word of mouth is described as "one of the most effective and lasting ways" to promote a site. — [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)

### Inferences
- Title patterns: home = "Noman E Jawad | Freelance Full Stack Developer (Next.js, React, Laravel)"; service = "Freelance Next.js Developer for Startups and SMEs | Noman E Jawad"; case study = "[Client/Project]: [outcome] with [stack] | Case Study"; post = the specific problem phrase. Keep the primary phrase near the front; roughly 50-60 characters to avoid truncation (convention, not a Google rule).
- Meta descriptions: one or two sentences stating who it is for, what he does, a proof point and location/timezone. Google rewrites descriptions often, so write for click-through, not keywords.
- One H1 per page containing the page's primary phrase in natural language; H2s that mirror the questions a client asks (scope, process, timeline, pricing, stack). This also produces extractable passages for AI answers.
- URL structure: `/work/[slug]`, `/services/[slug]`, `/blog/[slug]`, `/about`, `/contact`; lowercase, hyphenated, no dates in blog URLs.
- Internal linking: every case study links to the relevant service page and to related posts; every service page links to two or three proving case studies; posts link to the service they support. On a 7-30 page site this hub-and-spoke linking is the main lever for telling Google which page should rank for which term.
- The current single-page home with anchor sections gives Google only one URL to rank for everything; splitting services, about and contact into their own URLs creates more targeted entry points.
- E-E-A-T signals an individual can actually provide: real photo and full name; an About page with work history and location; named clients, live links and measurable outcomes in case studies; client testimonials with name, role and company (ideally linking to their LinkedIn); visible publish and update dates; a byline linking to About; `sameAs` links to GitHub, LinkedIn and npm that link back; clear contact details and business terms.
- Image alt text should describe what the screenshot shows ("Checkout page of the X store after the redesign"), not repeat keywords.

### Gaps
- No Google guidance specific to freelancer or portfolio sites exists; the patterns above are applications of general guidance.
- Title-length and description-length figures are industry convention and were not sourced in this research.

## 5. Content strategy, AI-generated content, and service/landing pages

### Takeaway
Google permits AI-assisted content but treats mass-generated, low-value pages as scaled content abuse and location-swapped pages as doorway abuse, with spam updates in June and August 2026 reinforcing this. For a one-person site the defensible plan is a small set of hand-built service pages, detailed case studies, and a low-frequency blog of first-hand technical and client-decision content; programmatic page generation is not appropriate at this scale.

### Cited Findings
- "Using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate Google's spam policy on scaled content abuse." "It is critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing." Generative AI "can be particularly useful when researching a topic, and to add structure to original content." Updated 1 Oct 2026. — [Google: Using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- "Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings." Doorway abuse: "sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries". The spam policies now also cover "attempting to manipulate generative AI responses in Google Search". — [Google spam policies, updated 28 Aug 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Google ran spam updates in late June 2026 and on 18-21 August 2026 (snippet only; secondary sources). — [GSQi: August 2026 spam update case studies](https://gsqi.com/marketing-blog/august-2026-google-spam-update-case-studies/); [AIOSEO: June 2026 spam update](https://aioseo.com/google-june-2026-spam-update/)
- Warning signs of search-engine-first content include producing content mainly to attract search visits, extensive automation across many topics, mainly summarising others' work, and changing dates to appear fresh without substantive updates. Google also asks whether the site explains how AI or automation was used. — [Google: Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Comparative queries ("best", "vs", "recommend") generated 2.4x more brand mentions in AI answers than informational content (Semrush, June 2026, 115 prompts, 3,981 domain appearances). — [Semrush ghost citations study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/the-ghost-citations-study/)
- Claim that a freelancer who writes only about one niche "will out-rank and out-recommend a generalist with a scattered portfolio" (freelancer-marketing blog, opinion, snippet only). — [SolidGigs](https://solidgigs.com/blog/7-smart-strategies-that-rank-your-portfolio-in-search-and-ai-chat/)

### Inferences
- Service pages work for freelancers when they are few, distinct and backed by proof. Suggested set of four to six: `/services/nextjs-development`, `/services/woocommerce-development` (or headless commerce), `/services/ai-integration`, `/services/3d-web-experiences`, `/services/laravel-development`, plus possibly one audience page (`/for/australian-businesses`). Each needs unique scope, process, indicative pricing or engagement model, two or three linked case studies, a testimonial and client-question answers. Generating dozens of stack-by-city permutations would fall under the doorway and scaled-content definitions.
- Topic focus: he lists five stacks plus AI plus 3D. Topical authority on a small site comes from depth in two or three intersecting clusters, not breadth. Likely strongest differentiators given low competition from individuals: (a) Next.js plus headless WordPress/WooCommerce, which uses both halves of his experience; (b) AI feature integration into existing business sites; (c) 3D/R3F as a showcase cluster. Generic "what is React" tutorials are both saturated and the query type most absorbed by AI Overviews.
- Blog topic types in priority order: (1) problem-solution posts from real client work (specific error, migration, performance fix, with code and before/after numbers); (2) client-decision content (cost of a Next.js site, WordPress vs Next.js, when headless WooCommerce is worth it, how to brief a freelance developer); (3) deep case-study write-ups; (4) notes on new releases he has actually used (Next.js 16 features). Types 1 and 4 build developer credibility and links; type 2 is what prospective clients search and what AI assistants quote.
- Case-study format: client and context, problem, constraints, approach and stack with reasons, his specific role, measurable result (Core Web Vitals, conversion, revenue, load time), testimonial, live link, screenshots with descriptive alt text, a "Need something similar?" link to the matching service page. Aim for several hundred words of real text each; thin gallery-only pages give Google nothing to rank.
- Cadence: Google states no frequency requirement. For one person, two solid posts a month (or one a month plus one case study a quarter) sustained for a year is more realistic and more valuable than a burst of AI-drafted posts. Update dates only when content substantively changes.
- AI use: drafting, outlining and editing with AI is within policy; publishing unreviewed AI text is the risk. The safe pattern is first-hand material (his code, metrics, screenshots, opinions) with AI as editor.

### Gaps
- No data was found on how many posts or what cadence a personal developer blog needs to gain traction; cadence advice is judgment.
- Details of what the June and August 2026 spam updates targeted come from secondary SEO blogs and were not confirmed against Google's own announcements.
- No evidence was found measuring whether freelancer service pages rank better than a single-page portfolio.

## 6. AI search / GEO: AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, llms.txt

### Takeaway
Google states there are no special requirements, files or schema for AI Overviews and AI Mode beyond being indexed and snippet-eligible, and both Google and Ahrefs data indicate llms.txt currently has no measurable effect. What correlates with AI visibility is being mentioned around the web (YouTube, third-party sites, communities), so for a freelancer "GEO" is mostly the same work as off-page reputation plus clear, quotable, well-structured pages.

### Cited Findings
- "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." Pages must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. AI features use "query fan-out", issuing multiple related searches across subtopics. Controls are `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, `noindex`. AI-feature traffic is counted in the Search Console Performance report under Web, not separated. — [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- Mueller (2026, Search Off the Record, reported by secondary sources; snippet only): llms.txt was not designed to make sites discoverable, is inherently untrustworthy as self-assertion, Google does not use llms.txt, and it may only help an agent already on a site navigate it. — [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/?p=19531); [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/googles-mueller-says-llmstxt-wont-guide-llm-recommendations-8afc3fa3)
- Ahrefs reportedly analysed 137,000 domains and found 97% of llms.txt files received zero requests in May 2026 (snippet only; the original Ahrefs page could not be located, and the accompanying "28% of domains published the file" figure is unverified). — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/google-flags-llmstxt-as-speculative-john-mueller-favors-webm-eaf6216b)
- Ahrefs, 75,000 brands (12 Dec 2025), Spearman correlations with AI visibility: YouTube mentions 0.737 (AI Overviews) / 0.740 (AI Mode) / 0.712 (ChatGPT); branded web mentions 0.656 / 0.709 / 0.664; branded anchors 0.527 / 0.628 / 0.511; branded search volume 0.392 / 0.466 / 0.352; Domain Rating 0.326 / 0.285 / 0.266; number of site pages about 0.194. Correlation only, and the sample was brands on domains with DR above 40, so applicability to a solo freelancer is indirect. — [Ahrefs: AI brand visibility correlations](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/)
- Semrush (9 Jun 2026): 62% of AI citations are "ghost citations" (source linked, brand not named); ChatGPT had an 87% citation rate but 20.7% mention rate, Gemini 83.7% mention rate but 21.4% citation rate; "different engines reward different signals, formats, and sources". — [Semrush ghost citations study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/the-ghost-citations-study/)
- Click impact: Ahrefs (Apr 2025, 300,000 keywords) found AI Overviews correlated with a 34.5% lower CTR for position 1. A May 2026 Ahrefs update reportedly raised this to 58% (snippet only). Seer Interactive reportedly measured about 3.3% CTR with no AI Overview, 2.1% when cited in one and 0.9% when not cited (snippet only). Pew (July 2025) reportedly found users clicked a result in 8% of visits with an AI summary versus 15% without (snippet only). — [Ahrefs: AI Overviews reduce clicks](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks/); [Search Engine Land on Seer study](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ctr-recovery-study-475566); [BusinessWire via Cadillac News](https://www.cadillacnews.com/ap/business/new-research-googles-ai-overviews-now-cost-websites-58-of-their-clicks/article_ee08befe-e058-53bc-9e2c-faed1fd43b84.html)
- Google's spam policies now cover attempts to manipulate generative AI responses (e.g. inauthentic mentions). — [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

### Inferences
- Practical GEO for this site: (1) be indexed in both Google and Bing (ChatGPT search is widely understood to draw on Bing's index, so Bing Webmaster Tools matters; not verified in this research); (2) do not block GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot or Google-Extended in robots.txt; (3) server-render all text, since AI crawlers generally do not execute JavaScript, which is another reason not to put key copy inside WebGL scenes; (4) state plain facts in extractable sentences on the home and About pages ("Noman E Jawad is a freelance full stack developer based in Dhaka, Bangladesh, specialising in Next.js, ...; works with clients in Australia and the US"); (5) keep name, title and description identical across the site, LinkedIn, GitHub, npm, dev.to and Upwork so engines resolve one entity; (6) earn third-party mentions (see question 7).
- An assistant answering "recommend a freelance Next.js developer" draws from marketplaces, listicles and directories far more than from individual portfolios. The realistic AI-search wins are name queries ("who is Noman E Jawad", "is X a legit developer") and citations of his technical posts for narrow how-to questions.
- llms.txt: optional. It takes minutes to add as a static file and does no harm, but evidence says it currently delivers nothing; it should sit at the bottom of the priority list and not be presented to the client as an SEO measure.
- Because informational queries lose the most clicks to AI Overviews, the blog should be valued for authority, links, citations and sales enablement (sending a post to a prospect), not for raw traffic.

### Gaps
- No study of AI-assistant visibility for individual freelancers or personal sites was found; all data concerns brands and companies.
- No primary confirmation was obtained of which crawlers or indexes ChatGPT and Perplexity use in 2026, or of the Ahrefs llms.txt and May 2026 CTR figures.
- YouTube mentions correlate most strongly with AI visibility, but whether a solo developer creating a few videos would benefit is untested.

## 7. Off-page signals: what moves the needle for a developer

### Takeaway
A handful of genuine links and mentions from relevant places (client sites, GitHub/npm projects, community posts, a few real directories and marketplaces) outweighs any volume tactic, and Google's spam policies rule out paid or scaled link schemes. Cross-posting to dev.to/Hashnode is worthwhile for reach and mentions, but Google does not treat rel=canonical on syndicated copies as reliable. Google Business Profile is essentially not applicable to a remote-only freelancer.

### Cited Findings
- Link spam is "creating links to or from a site primarily for the purpose of manipulating search rankings", including buying links, advertorial links that pass ranking credit, and forum comments with optimised links; paid links are acceptable only with `rel="nofollow"` or `rel="sponsored"`. — [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Syndication: "The canonical link element is not recommended for those who want to avoid duplication by syndication partners, because the pages are often very different. The most effective solution is for partners to block indexing of your content." — [Google: canonicalisation troubleshooting](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting)
- Google Business Profile requires a physical location customers can visit or travel to customers; a virtual office is not eligible; service areas should not extend beyond about 2 hours' driving time from the base. Secondary sources state online-only businesses are not eligible. — [Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177); [Whitespark GBP eligibility guide](https://whitespark.ca/guides/can-your-business-be-on-google-a-guide-to-gbp-eligibility-by-business-type/)
- Branded web mentions and branded anchors correlate with AI visibility far more than raw backlink counts (see question 6). — [Ahrefs: AI brand visibility correlations](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/)
- Pages getting organic traffic with no backlinks are almost all on low-competition topics; competitive first-page rankings without backlinks are rare. — [Ahrefs search traffic study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-traffic-study/)
- Mueller advises new sites to prioritise community-building and direct promotion ahead of search visibility. — [Search Engine Journal, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-mueller-free-subdomain-hosting-makes-seo-harder/565249/)

### Inferences
- Priority order by expected impact for one person:
  1. Client-site credit links ("Website by Noman E Jawad" in the footer or a credits page, where the client agrees) and testimonials on LinkedIn. These are editorially relevant, hard for competitors to copy and double as referral paths.
  2. Complete, consistent profiles that link to the site: GitHub profile and README, npm author field, LinkedIn (Featured section and contact info), dev.to, Hashnode, Upwork, Stack Overflow, X/Bluesky. Many of these links are nofollow, but they establish the entity for the branded SERP and for AI engines, and they are the pages that rank for his name alongside the site.
  3. One or two small open-source artefacts (a Next.js starter, R3F component, WooCommerce helper, or the portfolio template itself) with the site as homepage URL; successful ones attract natural links and mentions from lists and newsletters.
  4. Cross-posting each article to dev.to and Hashnode with the canonical URL set to his domain, published a few days after the original is indexed. Given Google's statement, the copy on a stronger domain can still outrank the original; that is acceptable when the goal is reach and mentions, and can be reduced by posting an excerpt with a "read the full post" link instead of the full text.
  5. Community participation where clients and peers actually are (relevant subreddits, Next.js/Three.js Discords and GitHub discussions, LinkedIn posts aimed at Australian SME owners). Reddit and YouTube content is disproportionately surfaced by AI engines.
  6. Selective directories and marketplaces: Clutch, GoodFirms, DesignRush, Sortlist, Contra, Upwork, and showcase galleries for 3D work (Awwwards, Three.js/R3F showcases, Vercel/Next.js showcase submissions). These are also the pages that actually rank for "hire X developer" terms, so appearing inside them is the realistic way to be present on those SERPs.
  7. Occasional guest posts or podcast/newsletter appearances on real developer or small-business publications, written for the audience rather than the link.
- Avoid: paid link packages, mass directory submissions, link exchanges, comment/forum signature links, private blog networks, and fake reviews or seeded mentions (now explicitly within spam policy even when aimed at AI answers).
- Google Business Profile: he does not meet customers in person and targets overseas clients, so a profile would breach the guidelines and risks suspension; a Dhaka listing would in any case surface for Dhaka-local searches, not Australian or US ones. Skip unless he genuinely serves local Dhaka clients in person.
- LinkedIn is probably the single most valuable off-page asset for his target clients (Australian and US business owners), as a lead channel in its own right more than as an SEO signal.

### Gaps
- No data was found quantifying the ranking effect of client footer links, GitHub/npm profile links or dev.to cross-posting for personal sites.
- Follow/nofollow status of profile links on GitHub, npm, dev.to, Hashnode and LinkedIn was not checked.
- No evidence was gathered on which directories send real leads to solo developers versus agencies.

## 8. Measurement and realistic timelines

### Takeaway
Set up Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools on day one of the custom domain, keep the existing Vercel Analytics, and judge progress by branded impressions, indexed pages, long-tail query growth and enquiries rather than head-term rankings. Expect indexing in days to weeks, branded visibility within about a month, first long-tail impressions in two to four months, and meaningful non-branded leads from search only after six to twelve months of sustained content and mentions, if at all.

### Cited Findings
- Google: some changes take effect in hours, others take several months; generally allow a few weeks before assessing. — [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
- Site moves: "a few weeks or more" for a medium-sized site to switch over; keep redirects at least 1 year; expect temporary fluctuation. The Search Console move notice reportedly displays for 180 days (secondary source, snippet only). — [Google: Site move with URL changes](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes); [Search Engine Journal on Change of Address](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-console-change-of-address/349560)
- One developer's reported timeline on a fresh host: first crawl in 2-3 days, main pages indexed in 1-2 weeks, rankings (previously held, mostly branded) returning in weeks 3-4, after Search Console verification, sitemap submission, manual indexing requests and import into Bing Webmaster Tools. Anecdotal. — [dev.to: From custom domain to Vercel subdomain](https://dev.to/imranparthib/from-custom-domain-to-vercel-subdomain-how-i-recovered-my-seo-rankings-56i7)
- Only 1.74% of new pages reach the top 10 within a year; the average #1 page is 5 years old. — [Ahrefs: How long does it take to rank](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/)
- AI Overview and AI Mode impressions and clicks are included in the Search Console Performance report under the Web search type, not broken out. — [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- SparkToro reportedly found 68% of US Google searches ended without a click in early 2026 (snippet only). — [TechWyse report](https://www.techwyse.com/news/reports-data/google-zero-click-searches-68-percent-2026)
- The project already added Vercel Analytics (repo commit "Add Vercel Analytics, upgrade Next.js to 16.2.9"). — local git history, no URL.

### Inferences
- Setup: Search Console Domain property (DNS TXT) for the new domain plus a URL-prefix property for the old vercel.app host (needed for Change of Address); submit `sitemap.xml`; request indexing for the home page, service pages and case studies. Bing Webmaster Tools can import from Search Console. Add IndexNow only if convenient. Keep Vercel Analytics for traffic and add Speed Insights or use the Search Console Core Web Vitals report once there is enough field data (a low-traffic site may never have CrUX data, so rely on Lighthouse/PageSpeed lab tests).
- Track conversions, not sessions: contact-form submissions, email and calendar-link clicks, and a "how did you find me?" field on the enquiry form, since AI-assistant and dark-social referrals are largely invisible in analytics.
- Monthly review: Search Console queries with impressions but positions 8-20 (candidates for a dedicated section or post), pages indexed versus submitted, branded versus non-branded clicks, new referring domains.
- Realistic 12-month expectation for a new domain with about 6 service pages, 6-10 case studies and 15-25 posts: ownership of the branded SERP; tens to low hundreds of non-branded clicks per month, mostly to technical posts; a small number of long-tail hire-intent rankings; occasional inbound enquiries. Search is unlikely to be the primary lead source in year one; the site's main commercial role is converting prospects who arrive from LinkedIn, Upwork, referrals and outreach. This is a judgment based on the Ahrefs time-to-rank data and SERP composition, not a measured forecast.
- Suggested impact ordering across the whole strategy: (1) custom domain and clean migration; (2) technical baseline (metadata, canonical, sitemap, robots, JSON-LD, OG images, static rendering, Core Web Vitals); (3) split the single page into service, about and contact pages and deepen the case studies; (4) Search Console and Bing setup; (5) consistent profiles and client credit links; (6) a sustained blog in two or three clusters with dev.to/Hashnode cross-posting; (7) directories, marketplaces and showcases; (8) optional extras such as RSS, FAQPage/Service schema and llms.txt.

### Gaps
- No benchmark data was found for traffic or lead volumes that freelance developer portfolios achieve from organic search.
- The current indexing status and any existing rankings of nomanjawad.vercel.app were not checked (requires Search Console access or a `site:` query).
- Third-party rank or AI-visibility tracking tools were not evaluated; for a site this small Search Console is likely sufficient.
